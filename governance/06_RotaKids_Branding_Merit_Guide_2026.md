1. Core Visual Identity

The  RotaKids  brand  is  the  bridge  between  childhood  and  community  service.  Its  visual

identity must be approachable and distinctive, while remaining firmly rooted in the Rotary

heritage.

a) Brand Color: Turquoise (PMS 7466C / Hex #00adbb / R0 G173 B187). This color is unique

within the Rotary family - it is not used in the Rotary, Interact, or Rotaract logos - symbolizing

freshness, energy, and the clarity of purpose that defines every RotaKid.

b)  Typography:  Open  Sans  is  the  official  RotaKids  typeface,  consistent  with  Rotary

International's  digital  brand  standards.  For  full  typography  guidelines,  refer  to  Annex  A  -

Visual Identity Style Guide.

2. The Gear of Service Merit System

The Three Wheels system represents the developmental journey of the RotaKids member.

Progress is not from "small to  large", but from "belonging" to "acting" and,  ultimately, to

"setting others in motion".

The Gear of Service is reflected through a single badge format using two marker systems:

Level Markers and Progress Markers. The color used to fill the circles is at the discretion of

the RotaKids club, subject to approval by the sponsoring Rotary Club, consistent with the

uniform color guidelines in Annex A.

LEVEL I - THE SOLID WHEEL Philosophy: "You are in the circle" (TO BE / BELONGING)

Significance: The stage of values assimilation. The child learns what it means to belong to a

team, to make a pledge, and to show up for others.

Progress Markers (3):

1)  First  Progress  Marker:  Awarded  upon  meeting  the  first  milestone  defined  by  the

District RotaKids Committee (example: recitation of the RotaKids Pledge and active

participation  in  the first  group  activity or  accumulation  of  the  minimum  volunteer

hours defined by the District for this milestone).

2)  Second Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the second milestone defined by

the District RotaKids Committee (example: completion of an individual contribution

to a club project or accumulation of the minimum volunteer hours defined by the

District for this milestone).

3)  Third Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the third milestone  defined by the

District  RotaKids  Committee  (example:  demonstrated  consistency  -  sustained

attendance  and  participation  over  the  defined  period  or  accumulation  of  the

minimum volunteer hours defined by the District for this milestone).

LEVEL II - THE SPOKED WHEEL Philosophy: "You are a spoke" (TO DO / DIRECTION)

Significance: The child now holds a specific role within the mechanism. They learn individual

responsibility and the value of coordinated effort.

Progress Markers (3):

1)  First  Progress  Marker:  Awarded  upon  meeting  the  first  milestone  defined  by  the

District  RotaKids  Committee  (example:  completion  of  an  individual  task  within  a

project or accumulation of the minimum volunteer hours defined by the District for

this milestone).

2)  Second Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the second milestone defined by

the  District  RotaKids  Committee  (example:  coordination  of  a  small  team  within  a

project or accumulation of the minimum volunteer hours defined by the District for

this milestone).

3)  Third Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the third milestone  defined by the

District  RotaKids  Committee  (example:  mentoring  a  new  Level  I  member  or

accumulation  of  the  minimum  volunteer  hours  defined  by  the  District  for  this

milestone).

LEVEL III - THE COGWHEEL Philosophy: "You are in the gear" (TO MULTIPLY / LEADERSHIP)

Significance:  The  capacity  to  move  other  mechanisms.  Final  preparation  for  transition  to

Interact and lifelong service within the Rotary family.

Progress Markers (3):

1)  First  Progress  Marker:  Awarded  upon  meeting  the  first  milestone  defined  by  the

District  RotaKids  Committee  (example:  initiating  and  planning  a  new  project  or

accumulation  of  the  minimum  volunteer  hours  defined  by  the  District  for  this

milestone).

2)  Second Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the second milestone defined by

the District RotaKids Committee (example: collaboration with the sponsoring Rotary

Club  or  local  authorities  on  a  project  or  accumulation  of  the  minimum  volunteer

hours defined by the District for this milestone).

3)  Third  Progress Marker: Awarded upon meeting the third milestone  defined by the

District RotaKids Committee (example: full completion of Level III requirements - the

Master Gear milestone or accumulation of the minimum volunteer hours defined by

the District for this milestone).

Each District RotaKids Committee is responsible for establishing the specific requirements

for each Progress Marker within its jurisdiction, whether defined as a minimum number of

volunteer hours, a specific type of activity, or a combination of both. These requirements

shall  be  documented

in  the  District's  supplementary

internal  regulations  and

communicated clearly to all sponsoring clubs.

3. Physical Badge Specifications

To ensure global accessibility and a zero cost barrier, this guide encourages local production

of badges using readily available materials:

-  Badge  Format:  Circular  disc,  diameter  at  the  discretion  of  the  club,  ensuring  all

graphic elements remain clearly legible. As a general guideline, 38mm is recommended for

everyday wear and 58mm for outdoor activities and events.

-  Fastening System: Safety pin (standard) or magnetic clasp (optional, recommended

where pin safety is a concern for younger members).

-  Marker Systems: Each badge contains two sets of circle positions: three Level Markers

at 9 o'clock and three Progress Markers at 3 o'clock. Clubs are encouraged to use any locally

available, low-cost material to fill the circle positions on the badge - including self-adhesive

rhinestone  crystals,  colored  stickers,  enamel  paint,  or  equivalent  materials.  No  specific

material is mandated globally. The estimated cost per badge unit should remain under USD

0.50, allowing any club in the world to implement the system without subsidies.

-  Design  Files:  Digital  templates  for  badge  production  are  available  for  download  at

rotakids.org following the adoption of this resolution.

4. Brand Protection & Digital Assets

The transfer of digital assets to Rotary International ensures that:

1.  rotakids.net - Remains the official global registry of verified RotaKids clubs.
2.  rotakids.org  -  Serves  as  the  central  library  for  this  guide  and  all  programme

handbooks and templates.

3.  rotakids.com - Provides the global fundraising and awareness platform for projects,

materials, and resources.

4.  rotakids.club - Provides each new club with a standardized digital identity, protecting

the brand from unauthorized use on external platforms.
