Chapter 0 - General provisions
Art. 0.1 - Purpose. This document proposes implementing rules for the RotaKids framework
proposed by Resolution 26R-11: the allocation of responsibilities, data management, operating
procedures and the specifications of the digital platform. It also answers questions raised by
Rotarians from several districts after the resolution package was published.
Art. 0.2 - Relationship with documents 01-10. This document complements documents 01-10
(01. Standard Constitution, 02. Standard Bylaws, 03. Statement of Policy, 04. Sponsor Guide, 05. Global
Handbook, 06. Branding & Merit Guide, 07. Safeguarding Framework, 08. Position Paper, 09. Visual
Guide - Annex A, 10. Project Showcase - Annex B). Where a provision of this document differs from
an earlier document, an amendment of the earlier document is proposed; the proposed
amendments are listed in Chapter 12.
Art. 0.3 - Application. The proposed rules would apply after the adoption of the resolution and
the decision of the RI Board, for the duration of the 3-year pilot proposed in Chapter 10. The
provisions are worded normatively for clarity, but all of them are proposals and may be amended
by the RI Board or by districts, within their respective responsibilities.

Chapter 1 - Allocation of responsibilities
Art. 1.1 - Levels. Responsibilities are proposed to be allocated on three levels: RI, the district and
the club. RI sets the minimum; the district may impose stricter rules, never more lenient ones. This
allocation follows the original model of the program (Florida, 2000), in which clubs operated in
schools, with an advisor from the school and a Rotary liaison, while the district supplied the
materials.

Art. 1.3 - Responsibility in the event of an incident. Operational responsibility lies with the
sponsor club (or clubs) and the district. In the school-based model, the school is responsible for
day-to-day protocols, under its agreement with the sponsor club. The sponsor club may take out
liability insurance in line with local practice.
Rationale: an internal policy cannot remove legal liability, which is determined by local law. This
rule sets out who acts, not who is exempted.
Art. 1.4 - A district that does not comply with safeguarding rules. The RI General Secretary
may suspend the district's authorization. During the suspension, the RotaKids clubs in that district
suspend their activities until the situation is remedied.
Rationale: the sanction applies only to the district concerned, not to the whole program.
Art. 1.5 - A sponsor club that ceases to operate.
1. The district informs the Rotary clubs in the district that there is a RotaKids club without a
sponsor.
2. For 180 days, the District RotaKids Chair acts as interim sponsor on behalf of the district.
3. During this period, activities are limited to those supervised by the school; new projects
involving travel or public events are suspended.
4. If a new sponsor takes over the club, activities resume normally.
5. If there is no sponsor after 180 days, the authorization is withdrawn. The school may
continue its relationship with the district as a Rotary Partner School, without the RotaKids
brand.
Rationale: the rule applies the same mechanism and the same time limit, both drawn from the
Standard Rotaract Club Constitution.

Art. 2.4 - Vetting of adults.
1. The platform records only: the type of check, who carried it out, the date it was carried out
and its expiry date.

2. The document itself (criminal record certificate, other certificate) stays with the district or
the sponsor club.
3. An adult account is activated only after validation by the District RotaKids Chair or, in
multinational districts, by the national coordinator of the country concerned.
4. 60 days before the check expires, the adult and the District RotaKids Chair are notified. On
expiry, the account is suspended until the check is renewed.

Art. 2.5 - The RI youth protection course. The “Protecting Youth Program Participants” course
in the RI Learning Center is recommended for every adult with an account. The adult declares in
their account that they have taken it, and the date. This information is visible only to the adult and
the District RotaKids Chair. The district may make the course mandatory.
Art. 2.6 - Public display of adults. Club websites show only the roles (Rotarian Advisor, Faculty
Advisor), without names. Contact is made only through the form (Art. 3.2).
Art. 2.7 - The district's configurable fields.
1. The district adds the fields required by its law or its own policy (for example, D2241:
criminal record certificate, certificate of behavioral integrity).
2. Permitted types: tick box, date, list of options, short text. File uploads are not permitted.
3. Fields may be added for adults and for clubs. No fields may be added for children.
4. A field may be marked as mandatory (it blocks account activation) or as expiring (Art. 2.4,
item 4 applies).
5. Fields are defined per district + country pair (Chapter 8).
Rationale: the platform contains no national legal rules. Each district applies the law of its country
and is responsible for doing so. The ban on fields for children preserves the principle in Art. 2.2.
Chapter 3 - Protection of minors on the platform
Art. 3.1 - Incident reporting.
1. Any allegation or suspicion of abuse or harassment is reported immediately to the local
authorities, in accordance with local law.
2. Within 24 hours, it is reported to the district Youth Protection Officer and the District
Governor.
3. Within 72 hours, it is reported to RI, through RI's youth protection reporting procedure.
4. The platform does not store incident reports.
RotaKids - Proposed Implementing Rules | Adrian Stan, RC Pitesti, D2241 | 6 / 21

Rationale: the Rotary Code of Policies requires all allegations of abuse or harassment to be
reported to RI within 72 hours. This rule is aligned with it and replaces the note in Document 07,
§5 (Chapter 12).
Art. 3.2 - Public contact. The contact form on the club website is addressed only to the Rotarian
Advisor / Faculty Advisor. Roles held by children (president, secretary, etc.) do not appear as
recipients.
Rationale: a form addressed to a child would publicly signal that the child can be contacted.
Art. 3.3 - Parental consent for images and names. The consent form signed on enrollment
contains the following options:
- group photos;
- individual portrait;
- no public photos (applies by default if nothing is ticked);
- publication of the first name.
The consent is valid from enrollment until it is withdrawn. The selected options exist only on the
paper form, in the local file.
Art. 3.4 - Publishing.
1. Only a responsible adult may publish.
2. Each time they publish, the adult must tick the declaration: “I have checked the consent forms
of the children identifiable in the images and I confirm that the images and any first names of
children appear in accordance with the options ticked by the parents.”
3. Responsibility for the published content lies with the adult who publishes it.
4. The platform keeps an internal log (who published what and when), which the District
RotaKids Chair can access at any time.
5. The platform does not automatically check the content of images and does not use facial
recognition.
Rationale: facial recognition would mean processing minors' biometric data. Individual
responsibility, documented by the log, is sufficient and proportionate.
Art. 3.5 - Withdrawal of consent.
1. The parent submits a written, signed request to the RotaKids club, to the responsible adult,
where the original consent was signed.
2. From the moment the request is received, no new images of the child are published.
3. The responsible adult updates the record on the platform and removes images already
published within 30 days at most.
4. The request is kept in the local file, alongside the original consent.
RotaKids - Proposed Implementing Rules | Adrian Stan, RC Pitesti, D2241 | 7 / 21

5. The request is not subject to approval; it is recorded.
Rationale: withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it (GDPR, Art. 7(3)). Submitting it in the
same place and in the same form respects this symmetry.
Art. 3.6 - Removal of a specific image. Every public image has a “Request image removal” button.
The image is hidden immediately, and the responsible adult reviews the request afterwards.
Art. 3.7 - Image metadata. On upload, the platform removes all metadata, including GPS
coordinates, and does not keep the original file (technical specification in Art. 11.4).
Rationale: photos taken with a phone may contain the location where they were taken.
Art. 3.8 – Supervision of activities
1. No adult is left alone with a child.
2. In the community-based model, at least two vetted adults are present at every activity
(Document 07, §3B).
3. In the school-based model, the school's rules apply, subject to item 1.
4. The district or national law may require the presence of two adults in the school-based
model as well.
Rationale: RI requires adults to avoid unsupervised one-on-one contact with young people. The
two-adult rule is an additional requirement, applicable under Art. 1.1.
Chapter 4 - Club life cycle
Art. 4.1 - Chartering. On chartering, the club receives a new club ID. An ID is never reused or
reactivated, not even for a reactivated club.
Rationale: a unique ID prevents the histories of two different clubs from being mixed.
Art. 4.2 - Annual update window. Between May 1 and July 31, the responsible adult updates the
following on the platform:
1. the RotaKids committee for the next Rotary year (aliases and roles);
2. the responsible adults: each one is reconfirmed (“stays on next year”) or replaced;
3. the sponsor club's contact details for the new Rotary year: the name and email of the
president and of the secretary.
A club that has completed its update is considered active. The update cannot be saved if an adult
(new or reconfirmed) has an expired integrity check.
Rationale: the annual change of committee is needed anyway (for example, for the president's
certificate), so the same operation confirms that the club is operating. Reconfirming the adults
checks that the responsible adult is still present.

Art. 4.3 - The July 1 transition. On July 1 at 00:00 (the district's local time), the committee and
adults entered for the new year become active and the previous ones move to the history.
Messages from the contact form are automatically redirected to the new office holders.
Art. 4.4 - Failure to update.
Date Action

June 15, July 15 Reminder to the responsible adults
July 31 Notice to the responsible adults and to the president of the sponsor club,
with an endorsement link
August 31 Notice to the District RotaKids Chair
Art. 4.5 - Endorsement by the sponsor club. The president of the sponsor club may confirm,
through a unique link received by email and without an account, that the RotaKids club is
operating even though it has not been updated. The endorsement:
1. is valid until the next update window (at most 1 year);
2. does not cover an adult with an expired integrity check;
3. transfers responsibility for the club's status to the sponsor club.
Art. 4.6 - A second year without an update. If the update is not made in the following period
either, the District RotaKids Chair proposes to the Governor that the authorization be withdrawn,
and the Governor decides.
Art. 4.7 - Closed club.
1. The club's website switches to archive mode (read-only) at the permanent address
rotakids.club/{club ID}.
2. The slug (for example /pitesti) is released. Until another club takes it, it redirects to the
archive.
3. When a new club takes the slug, its page shows a reference to the archive of the previous
club with the same name.
4. The club's own domain is no longer served by the platform after 90 days. The decision on
the domain rests with the sponsor club.
Rationale: the children's projects remain available and old links (press, social media) are not lost.
Releasing the slug allows a future club to use the name.

Art. 5.1 - Website address. Each club automatically has two addresses:
1. rotakids.club/{slug} - the club's name, allocated in order of request; where names coincide,
the town or state is added (for example /springfield-il);
2. rotakids.club/{club ID} - the permanent address, which redirects to the slug while the club
is active.
Art. 5.2 - Own domain. A club may use its own domain (for example rotakidspitesti.ro or rk-
evanston.org etc.), without the www prefix.
1. The club buys the domain from the national registrar.
2. The club sets up at the registrar the DNS records indicated by the platform (Art. 11.1).
3. The responsible adult enters the domain in my.rotakids.org.
4. The own domain becomes the primary address; rotakids.club/{slug} and www. redirect
permanently (301) to it.
Rationale: a single canonical address avoids duplicate content and preserves the club's local
identity.
Art. 5.3 - Website structure. The header (the official logo) and the footer (safeguarding
information and contact) are fixed and identical for all clubs. The page content is built from
predefined blocks: about the club, projects, gallery, history, contact, etc. Projects are articles (text
and images) and are not registered separately on the platform.
Rationale: the visual identity stays compliant with the brand guide, whoever edits the site.
Art. 5.4 - Children's contributions through a temporary link.
1. The responsible adult creates a new article and generates a contribution link.
2. The adult sets the link's validity: 24 hours by default, 7 days at most.
3. The children open the link without an account, write the text and upload images: at most
10 images, of at most 10 MB each.
4. The contribution is saved only as a draft. Nothing becomes public through the link.
5. The adult signs in, reviews the draft, may select the images, may edit the text and then save
the draft, publish it or delete the article (deletion requires confirmation).
6. If the link circulates outside the club, the adult closes it from their account.
Rationale: the children write about their projects themselves, without having accounts. Control
over publication stays with the adult.
Art. 5.5 - What is not published is deleted. Images that are not selected are deleted on
publication. Unpublished drafts are deleted automatically after 30 days.
Rationale: data minimization; the platform does not keep unpublished material.

Art. 5.6 - Email relay.
1. A visitor's message sent through the form is saved in the club's inbox and forwarded to the
personal email of the recipient adult.
2. The displayed sender is the club, and the adult's personal address is not visible to the
visitor.
3. The adult can reply directly from their email application; the platform verifies the sender,
archives the reply and sends it to the visitor on behalf of the club.
4. The conversation history stays with the club and passes to the new office holders on July
1.
Rationale: no personal address is published, and the club's communication is not lost when people
change.
Chapter 6 - Recognition and certificates
Art. 6.1 - The District RotaKids Citation. The Citation may be awarded only to clubs that meet at
least 5 of the 7 objectives in the Global Handbook, §9. Completing the three annual projects (local,
regional or national, international) is one of the 7 objectives. The fundraising project (objective 7)
is reported on the club's website. The district may adapt the criteria, with the approval of the
Governor or the District RotaKids Chair. There are no restrictions on clubs collaborating on
projects. The district assesses clubs on the basis of the projects section of their website.
Rationale: projects are not registered in a structured way on the platform (Art. 5.3), so the
assessment is made by the district, in accordance with Statement of Policy 13.090.
Art. 6.2 - The Jane Mills Award. A district award - the recipient or recipients are chosen by the
District RotaKids Chair, with the consent of the Governor. The criteria and the manner of awarding
are to be set later by each district.
Rationale: The award bears the name of Rotarian Jane Mills (Rotary Club of Stuart, Florida), a
teacher, founder of the first RotaKids club (September 2000) and, for more than a decade,
RotaKids Chair of District 6930.
Art. 6.3 - The child's certificates.
These provisions apply to all certificates issued to children: level, graduation, Master of the Gear,
office.
1. The responsible adult requests the certificate on the platform for the child's alias.
2. The platform generates a preprinted PDF showing: the district, the RotaKids club, the
school, the sponsor club, and the verification series and number (Art. 6.5).
3. The child's name, the period and the level achieved are filled in by hand, at the club.
4. The certificate is signed by the responsible adult and by the president of the sponsor club.

5. The child's alias does not appear on the certificate.
Rationale: the platform does not know the child's name. The alias is left off the certificate because
the certificate may be photographed and published; name and alias together would publicly link
the child to their history on the platform.
Art. 6.4 - The adult's certificate of participation. The platform issues a certificate of
participation for the Rotarian Advisor or Faculty Advisor, with their printed name, role and period,
and a verification series and number. The certificate is signed by the Governor or by the District
RotaKids Chair. It attests participation only; any professional recognition (for example by school
authorities) is negotiated locally by the district.
Art. 6.5 - Certificate verification.
1. The series is the district number at the date of issue (for example D2241).
2. The number is a random code of 8 characters followed by 1 check character (for example
K7QM4RXTJ), unrelated to the alias or to the database ID.
3. The series and number are entered at rotakids.org/verify; the certificate also carries a QR
code that fills them in automatically.
4. The page shows only: the status (valid, canceled, non-existent), the date of issue, the club,
the sponsor club, the district and the level recorded at issue.
5. The page never shows the name or the alias.
6. The series remains the one at issue even if the district is later renumbered or reorganized.
Rationale: verification confirms the authenticity of the document, not the identity of its holder.
Showing the recorded level makes any handwritten change on the certificate visible. The code
specification is in Art. 11.2.
Art. 6.6 - The Gear of Service badge. The badge has three levels: TO BE (solid wheel), TO DO
(spoked wheel), TO MULTIPLY (gear wheel). The badge contains no Rotary Marks and must not
resemble the Rotary emblem: a number of spokes other than 6, a number of teeth other than 24,
no keyway in the center, in turquoise or white, not gold and royal blue. Within these rules, the
badge may be produced freely, including by the children themselves (for example, from
cardboard). Gear of Service is the program's progression system (Statement of Policy 13.050). For
existing clubs, including those in the network developed by RIBI, introducing it is an addition to
their current activity, not a change to their structures.
Rationale: products bearing Rotary Marks may be made only by RI licensed vendors (Rotary Brand
Center; RI Code of Policies, provisions on the licensing of Rotary Marks). A badge without Rotary
Marks does not fall under this rule.

Art. 6.7 - Requested exception for materials bearing the logo. The RI Board is asked to grant
the following exception: badges, certificates and materials bearing the official RotaKids logo,
produced by clubs or sponsors for their own members, free of charge and not for sale, may be
made locally without a licensed vendor, in accordance with the RotaKids visual guide. Materials
that are sold or distributed for promotional purposes remain subject to the usual RI licensing
rules.
Art. 6.8 – District RotaKids Fellow.
1. A district distinction (for example “D2241 RotaKids Fellow”), awarded to individuals or
organizations that donate to RotaKids projects, in money or in goods.
2. The value thresholds and any grades are set by each district, in the national currency.
3. The donation is entered in the accounts of the sponsor club, which confirms it to the
district.
4. The distinction is awarded by the District RotaKids Chair, with the consent of the Governor.
5. The certificate has a verification series and number (Art. 6.5).
Rationale: thresholds differ between countries; a distinction with district thresholds is fair only if it
is a district distinction too.
Chapter 7 - Financial arrangements
Art. 7.1 - Dues. Children pay no dues and are not asked for any financial contribution (Standard
Constitution, Art. 8; Bylaws, Art. 7.1).
Art. 7.2 - Club funds.
1. Small amounts (for example from fundraising activities) are kept in the club's cash box, at
the school, under the responsibility of the responsible adult.
2. Large amounts and donations are managed through the account of the sponsoring Rotary
club and are entered in its accounts.
3. The amounts referred to in items 1 and 2 are set by the District RotaKids Chair, with the
consent of the Governor.
4. The club's annual financial statement is prepared on paper and kept in the local file.
5. The platform keeps no financial records.
Art. 7.3 - Uniforms and protocol materials. Funding for uniforms and the protocol kit is decided
locally, by the sponsor club and the district. The platform does not generate expense claims.
Art. 7.4 - Project support on rotakids.com. The module is optional. Each club decides whether
to activate it and in what form: donations in money, donations of goods, or both.

Art. 7.5 - Donations in money.
1. The project page shows the target, the amount raised (updated by the responsible adult)
and the sponsor club's donation details: the bank account or a link to the sponsor club's
donation page.
2. The donor pays the sponsor club directly.
3. The platform does not process payments.
4. The money donation module is activated only if the sponsor club is a legal entity and has a
dedicated bank account. The responsible adult confirms these conditions on activation;
responsibility lies with the sponsor club.
Rationale: a platform that processes payments become a payment service provider, with
obligations for customer checks, disputes and tax receipts in every country. Displaying the
donation details produces the same result without those obligations.
Art. 7.6 - Donations of goods.
1. The responsible adult publishes the list of materials needed for the project.
2. The donor buys and ships the item only to an institutional address: the school office or the
sponsor club's premises. Delivery to the address of a child or a parent is not permitted.
3. The responsible adult marks the item as received.
4. Vouchers with a monetary value are not entered on the platform; they are donated directly
to the sponsor club.
Art. 7.7 - Local restrictions. The club may activate the option “accept donations only from my
country” if local law restricts donations from abroad. Compliance with local rules on collections
and donations rests with the sponsor club.
Rationale: some national laws (for example the FCRA in India) also apply to goods received from
abroad, not only to money.
Chapter 8 - Jurisdictions and multinational districts
Art. 8.1 - Country and district. The country and the district are separate attributes of the club.
1. The country is given by the place where the club operates and does not change.
2. The district has an internal ID on the platform. The district's Rotary number (for example
2241) is an attribute with a start date and an end date.
3. A club's membership of a district is kept as history (for example: club 1234 in D2241 from
2014 to 2031).
Rationale: Rotary districts merge, split and are renumbered. Separating the two attributes allows
reorganizations without loss of history.

Art. 8.2 - Field sets. The configurable fields (Art. 2.7) are defined per district + country pair. A club
sees the set corresponding to its current district and its country.
Art. 8.3 - The national coordinator. In a multinational district, the Governor appoints a national
RotaKids coordinator for each country. The coordinator defines the field set for their country,
reviews the files of clubs in that country before certification and validates the accounts of adults
in that country.
Rationale: validation must be carried out by someone who knows the legislation of the country
concerned.

Art. 8.5 - Grace period. When a new mandatory field appears after a reorganization, adults have
180 days to complete it. The account is not suspended during this period. Data in the old fields
remains in the history.
Art. 8.6 - Legal assessment before launch. Before the platform is launched in a new jurisdiction,
the data processor and the district concerned review at least:
1. the rules on online contributions by children under 13 (for example COPPA in the US), for
the contribution link (Art. 5.4);
2. the obligations of the platform operator if illegal content is uploaded;
3. local rules on collections and donations (Art. 7.7);
4. the vetting requirements for adults working with children.
Rationale: this document makes no legal statements country by country. The outcome of the
review is reflected in the field set and in the district's settings.

Chapter 9 - Moving on to Interact
Art. 9.1 - Admission rule. Admission to Interact follows the bylaws of the Interact club receiving
the candidate. RotaKids membership does not confer an automatic right of admission.
Rationale: each Interact club adopts its own bylaws, which are approved by the sponsoring Rotary
club (Standard Interact Club Constitution). Neither RI nor the RotaKids program can directly set
the admission rules of another program.
Art. 9.2 - Model clause for RotaKids graduates. The following clause is made available to Interact
clubs for inclusion in their bylaws:
“RotaKids graduates who have completed at least one Gear of Service level may be admitted: (A) directly,
without a vote; or (B) by vote, without a prospective member period; or (C) by vote, with a prospective
member period reduced to [X] days/months.”
Art. 9.3 - District policy. The district may adopt, normally at the district assembly, a policy
recommending that all Interact clubs in the district include the clause, in a single variant (A, B or
C). The policy is applied through the sponsoring Rotary clubs, which approve the Interact bylaws.
Rationale: a child who moves to another town in the same district benefits from the same rule.
The mechanism encourages rather than obliges, like the proposal in Resolution 26R-06 on moving
from Rotaract to Rotary.
Art. 9.4 - Candidates from another district or another country. The rules of the district
receiving the candidate apply. The Interact club checks the candidate's RotaKids record at
rotakids.org/verify, on the basis of the certificate (Art. 6.5).
Art. 9.5 - Moving-up ceremony. The sponsoring Rotary club is encouraged to hold a graduation
ceremony and to make the connection with an Interact club. Members who have completed level
III receive the mention “Master of the Gear” on their certificate.

Chapter 11 - Technical specifications
Art. 11.1 - Own domains.
1. The primary address is the domain without www (for example rk-evanston.org).

2. The club sets up at the registrar A (IPv4) and AAAA (IPv6) records pointing to the platform's
fixed IP addresses. A CNAME record cannot be used on the domain without www (a
limitation of the DNS standard).
3. www.{domain} redirects permanently (301) to the domain without www.
4. rotakids.club/{slug} redirects permanently (301) to the own domain.
5. The platform recognises the domain from the HTTP request and serves the club's website.
6. The platform's IP addresses are stable anycast addresses and do not change without 12
months' notice to clubs with their own domain.
Art. 11.2 - Certificate verification code.
Parameter Value
Alphabet 32 symbols: the letters A-Z without I and O (24) and the digits 2-9 (8)
Length 8 random characters + 1 check character
Check character Calculated from the first 8 characters (Luhn mod 32 algorithm)
Generation Cryptographic random number generator; the code is not derived
from the alias or the ID
Uniqueness Global, across the whole platform
Entry No hyphens; lower-case letters are accepted
Excluded character Explicit message: “the character X is not used in codes”
entered (I, O, 0, 1)
Wrong check character Message: “code entered incorrectly”, distinct from “certificate does
not exist”
Rate limiting Maximum number of queries per hour per IP address
The code space is approximately 10¹² combinations.
Art. 11.3 - Contribution link.
1. Form: rotakids.club/{slug}/contribution/{token}; on an own domain:
{domain}/contribution/{token}.
2. The token has at least 128 random bits and is stored on the server only as a hash.
3. The link expires on the date set by the adult (24 hours by default, 7 days at most) and can
be closed at any time.
4. Limits: at most 10 images, of at most 10 MB each.
5. Images go through the pipeline in Art. 11.4 right from upload.
Art. 11.4 - Image processing pipeline.
1. Automatic rotation according to the orientation recorded by the camera.
2. Removal of metadata (EXIF, XMP, IPTC), including GPS coordinates.
3. Resizing to display dimensions.

4.  Conversion to JPEG or WebP (including from HEIC).
5.
The original file is not kept.
6.  On every deployment, an automated test runs a sample image with GPS coordinates
through the pipeline and checks that the result no longer contains metadata.
Rationale for the order: if metadata is removed before rotation, the orientation information is lost
and the image may appear upside down or sideways.

Art. 11.5 - Internal log. For each publication and deletion, the platform records: the account, the
date and time, and the object affected. The log is accessible to the District RotaKids Chair.
