# Case Study: District 2241

## The Pioneer Model: RotaKids Pitesti and 12 Years of Institutional Continuity

## Beyond a Project: A Sustainable Movement

RotaKids Pitesti, Romania, provides evidence that the programme can become more than a one-year project or a single-person initiative.

Chartered on 23 March 2014, following a development phase that began in 2012, the club has operated continuously across successive generations of children, mentors, and Rotary leadership.

For more than 12 years, the club maintained a regular weekly rhythm: members met every Sunday during the school year, except during official school holidays, while also participating in community-service projects, public events, educational activities, and Rotary-family initiatives.

This serves as a proof of concept for the 2026 RotaKids Resolution.

## Institutional Stability

The club has successfully passed from its founding local leadership to new RotaKids chairs.

This demonstrates that RotaKids can function as a sustainable, intergenerational Rotary-family programme rather than as a temporary local experiment dependent on a single individual.

## The Youth Pipeline

Internal tracking data shows that more than two-thirds of RotaKids members in Pitesti continue their Rotary journey through Interact and later Rotaract.

RotaKids therefore serves as a practical early entry point into the Rotary family and contributes to long-term continuity across successive youth programmes.

## Official Charter

**RotaKids Pitesti Charter:** 23 March 2014

The formal charter followed a development phase that began in 2012 and the first RotaKids meetings held in autumn 2013.

## A Lived Memory of Service

> By the time a child reaches the age of 12 in the Pitesti model, their perception of community and service is already shaped. They do not simply “join” Rotary later; they grow up as part of the Rotary family, making Service Above Self a core part of their identity.

## Digital Foundation and Pioneering Phase

The domain `RotaKids.ro` was registered on 12 November 2012.

This marked the beginning of a two-year foundation period involving extensive board sessions at the Rotary Club of Pitesti and the drafting of the initial governing documents.

The first RotaKids meetings began in autumn 2013, establishing the practical framework before the formal chartering of the club in March 2014.

## Why This Case Matters

The RotaKids Club Pitesti case does not prove that every implementation will achieve the same results.

It proves that the model can:

- survive leadership change;
- operate safely over time;
- maintain institutional continuity;
- support successive generations of children;
- create a measurable pathway into Interact and Rotaract;
- function as part of the wider Rotary-family ecosystem.

## Evidence for the 2026 Resolution

The experience of District 2241 supports the central purpose of the 2026 RotaKids Resolution: to provide a common framework for a programme that is already capable of continuity, transition, and long-term service impact when supported by a Rotary club and district.

### Proven Scalability

The programme expanded from one club into a national network.

### Proven Transition

Successive generations of RotaKids members continued into Interact and Rotaract, with former participants later assuming leadership roles.

### Institutional History

The programme has more than 12 years of uninterrupted institutional history, with the formal club charter dating from 23 March 2014 and the development process beginning in 2012.

### Proven Safety

No safeguarding incidents were formally reported to the District 2241 RotaKids Committee during the documented period.
